PROBLEM

The state pays for medicine — but nobody knows if it is taken

3.4M

people in Kazakhstan received free outpatient medicines in 2025 — 1 in 6 residents

~50%

of patients who received free blood-pressure medicine did not use it

~2M

people aged 65+ — the patients most likely to forget doses

Plus: handwritten prescriptions nobody can read, 10-page leaflets nobody reads, and no idea where the medicine is cheaper.

Sources: Ministry of Health of Kazakhstan, May 2026 · National Center for Public Health analysis 2023 (via Tengri Health, Oct 2025) · Bureau of National Statistics, 1 Jan 2026

SOLUTION

From the doctor’s QR code to the patient’s “Taken”

  1. Doctor writes the prescription online → QR code
  2. Patient scans it → schedule for the whole course, offline reminders, one “Taken” button
  3. Family is notified if a dose is missed for 60 minutes
  4. Doctor sees adherence: % taken and missed doses

Value: the treatment the doctor prescribed actually happens — and everyone can see it.

BUSINESS MODEL

Free for patients. Paid by those who gain from adherence

1 · FIRST

Pharmacy chains

3% commission on orders from eRecept prescriptions · Chains hold 76% of pharmacy turnover

2 · NEXT

Pharma companies

Adherence programs: 500 ₸ (~$1) per active patient per month · Medisafe’s model (Sanofi-led $30M round)

3 · LATER

Health system

Per-patient fee to measure whether free medicine is taken: 500 ₸ per patient per month

Prices are our hypotheses — the pilot will show which payer converts first and at what price. No drug advertising (Health Code, art. 56).

GO-TO-MARKET

Doctors bring patients, patients bring their families

1 · Polyclinics

Every QR the doctor prints is a new patient. Sell to the clinic once — patients come for free

2 · Family invites

Each patient invites a relative with a 6-digit code — built-in word of mouth

3 · Pharmacy chains

QR posters at the counter for patients with paper prescriptions

CAC per clinic: ~200,000 ₸ (onboarding, training)

CAC per patient: ~700 ₸ (≈ 300 patients per clinic) — estimate
